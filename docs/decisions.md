# Decisions

- 2026-09-26 — ASR is `Qwen3-ASR-1.7B` + `Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B` instead of Whisper large-v3. Whisper is no longer the most accurate open model in 2026, and its word timestamps drift by roughly half a second, which is what merged several speakers into one subtitle.
- 2026-09-26 — Timing never comes from the LLM. Words are force-aligned locally; the translator only fills fixed cues and cannot merge or split them.
- 2026-09-26 — Speaker changes are detected from pauses, sentence ends and video shot changes before diarization is added, because edited montages change speaker on every cut with no pause.
- 2026-09-26 — Heavy engines (`qwen_asr`, `google.genai`) are imported inside the functions that use them, so tests, rendering and segmentation run without the models installed. Ruff rule PLC0415 is disabled for this reason.
- 2026-09-26 — Breaks: shot cut, sentence end, or a pause of 2.5 s inside an unfinished sentence. Shorter pauses keep a sentence together ("Longer than … that.").
- 2026-09-26 — Pieces shorter than 0.9 s are joined with a neighbour. Across a shot cut they become a two-line dialogue subtitle with dashes; otherwise the same speaker continues. Gemini hears the audio and collapses a dialogue back to one line when both parts are the same person.
- 2026-09-26 — `gemini-2.5-flash` was withdrawn for new keys; the translator falls back through the 3.x Flash models on 503 errors.
- 2026-09-26 — Subtitles are rendered as ASS sized from the real video resolution (font 5.8% of the short side, portrait videos get a higher bottom margin) instead of a fixed SRT style.
- 2026-09-26 — Word timing moved from `Qwen3-ForcedAligner` to CTC forced alignment with `MahmoudAshraf/mms-300m-1130-forced-aligner` (1130 languages, text romanised with `uroman`). The Qwen aligner put words inside silence (a subtitle at 0:14 for speech at 0:16.6) and let extra words push later ones late; CTC models silence explicitly with its blank token.
- 2026-09-26 — Three guards on top of CTC, because the MMS model hears quick street speech weakly: letters are forbidden inside silence found by Silero VAD; each word is softly kept within ±1 s of its sentence's rough Gemini time; a word whose letters scatter across a pause keeps the side holding most letters, and no word lasts longer than 0.3 s + 0.1 s per letter. Measured on the sample: no subtitle in silence, coverage 28/28.
- 2026-09-26 — In a two-speaker subtitle the second line appears when its speaker starts talking. The first line keeps its position because the second line's space is reserved while hidden (ASS alpha), so nothing jumps.
- 2026-09-26 — Vocal separation (Demucs) was tried and dropped: it did not make the aligner hear the weak voices any better on the sample.
- 2026-09-26 — Listening runs three Gemini passes and keeps a sentence only when another pass confirmed at least a third of its words at about the same time. Voting is word by word because passes split sentences differently. A single pass invented a "Nice." at 0:12 that no other pass heard, and that phantom is what put a subtitle on screen before the speaker at 0:13. Precision is preferred over faint lines.
- 2026-09-26 — Timing has a second opinion. When the CTC aligner puts a sentence more than 0.3 s outside its voted time and Qwen3-ASR heard the same words inside it, the Qwen times are used ("Nice." 18.92 → 17.92). Measured on the sample: every checkable subtitle starts within 0.1 s of the speech, none in silence.
- 2026-09-26 — A subtitle never runs past a shot cut into the next person's shot. A reaction and the next person's line are joined across a cut only when the whole exchange lasts 2 s or less. Linger after speech is 0.4 s, minimum display 0.8 s.
- 2026-09-26 — The voted listening result and the raw passes are cached in `heard.json`, so re-timing does not call Gemini again.
- 2026-09-27 — Any target language through `--to <code>`. A language table sets direction, font, line length and reading speed per language (Netflix-style: 42 chars and 17 cps by default, Chinese 16/9, Japanese 13/4, Thai and CJK break between characters). Arabic keeps its proven Arabic prompt with dialects; every other language uses one English prompt. Right-to-left marks are added only for right-to-left scripts.
- 2026-09-27 — Long videos: listening runs in pieces of about 5 minutes cut in the middle of a detected silence, and translation runs in 5-minute batches, each with its own audio slice and times relative to it. Speaker labels are prefixed per piece so two pieces never share a speaker by accident.
- 2026-09-27 — Links are downloaded with yt-dlp inside tarjim; audio-only inputs produce an .srt only.
- 2026-09-27 — `tarjim-serve` replaces the old browser listener on the same port 127.0.0.1:17653. It accepts links and uploaded files with a target language, runs one job at a time (one GPU), and guards every request: Host must be local (DNS rebinding), a token is required except for /ping and /languages, uploads must be media files under 8 GB with sanitised names, and only a job's own outputs can be downloaded.
- 2026-09-27 — Keys and tool paths live in `~/.tarjim/config.json` (or environment variables), never in the project.
- 2026-09-27 — Measured: the free Gemini tier allows 20 requests per day per model. Daily exhaustion now skips to the next model at once, per-minute limits wait the delay Google returns, and when every model is spent tarjim says so. A one-minute clip costs about 5 requests, a 12-minute video about 15, so the free tier covers a few videos a day, not a public service.
- 2026-09-27 — Browser extension rebuilt inside tarjim (`extension/`, Manifest V3, no build step). One page serves as the popup and as a full page: the popup translates the current tab, the full page takes a dropped file or a pasted link, because a popup closes when it loses focus and would cut a long upload. Right-click on any link or video translates in the last chosen language. Copy lives in `_locales` (Arabic and English), the layout follows the language direction, and titles in another script keep their punctuation (`unicode-bidi: plaintext`).
- 2026-09-27 — Design: calm and professional (PRODUCT.md). Restrained colour with one accent, the classic subtitle yellow, used only for the main action, focus and progress; neutrals tinted toward it; theme follows the system; system fonts only so nothing loads from outside. Progress shows the real stages, never an invented percentage; the only percentage is the upload.
- 2026-09-27 — Failures carry a reason code (`quota`, `key`, `download`, `tools`) that the extension turns into a plain sentence, and a failed job can be retried. The token field is masked.
- 2026-09-27 — The models folder is a setting (`models` in `~/.tarjim/config.json`) applied at startup; without it the server tried to download the aligner from the internet and stalled.
- 2026-09-27 — Dubbing moved into tarjim (`tarjim/dub/`) and improved over the old tool: every speaker is cloned from their own voice (samples cut per speaker from tarjim's word timings, split at pauses so no other voice leaks in), the original voices are removed with Demucs so music and ambience stay at full level instead of the old 12% background, and each line starts exactly where its speaker starts and can never run into the next speaker (speed-up to 1.8x, then a short fade).
- 2026-09-27 — Three engines as before: local cloning (XTTS-v2, CPML non-commercial licence), studio voices (edge-tts, sends the translated text to Microsoft) and Fish (sends a voice sample and the text; temporary private voices are deleted afterwards; the saved narrator voice is supported). An engine that cannot speak the language falls back to one that can; no key falls back to local cloning.
- 2026-09-27 — XTTS measured on very short lines produced up to 2.6 s for "إيه". Now: temperature 0.5, silence trimmed, and a take longer than the text needs is regenerated (3 tries, shortest kept). Numbers are spelled out for the voice only (num2words), Arabic gets diacritics (CATT) for clone and Fish; subtitles keep digits.
- 2026-09-27 — Verified on the sample by transcribing the dubbed audio back with Qwen3-ASR: the Arabic text is recovered nearly word for word for both engines, silence stays at background level (-50 dB) and speech sits at about -19 dB.
- 2026-09-27 — tarjim is now the base on this machine: the existing startup watchdog (`translate-listener.vbs` → `keepalive.ps1`) starts `tarjim.server` with pythonw on 127.0.0.1:17653 instead of the old listener (old script kept as `keepalive.old.ps1`). The old extension keeps working through the legacy `/translate` route, including dubbing. Without a console the server logs to `~/.tarjim/server.log`. `scripts/windows/copy-token.cmd` copies the token for pairing the new extension.
- 2026-09-27 — People who install tarjim bring their own keys from the extension: a first-run screen asks for a Gemini key when none is set (with the free Google AI Studio link), and Settings accepts Gemini and Fish keys. The server checks the key's shape, verifies a Gemini key with Google (listing models, no quota used) before saving, stores it owner-only, and only ever reports whether a key is set, never the key.
- 2026-09-27 — Target language, style, output and voice are remembered the moment they change, so the right-click menu uses the language picked in the popup even without pressing Translate. The Fish "calm Arabic narrator" voice is offered only for Arabic. Verified that dubbing speaks the target language: a French dub transcribed back as French.
- 2026-09-27 — Engines are the user's choice, per role. Listening: Gemini (three passes voted), OpenAI (`gpt-4o-transcribe-diarize`, speakers and times) or local (Qwen3-ASR on the device). Translation: Gemini (hears the audio), OpenAI, Anthropic Claude, or a local model through Ollama (`aya-expanse:8b` by default). Claude and local models cannot hear, so they get no audio and the prompt does not ask them to listen, and they never merge two speakers' lines. `openai_base_url` lets any OpenAI-compatible service be used (DeepSeek, Groq, OpenRouter). Timing stays local for every engine.
- 2026-09-27 — The local engine backs up the chosen one: if Gemini's quota runs out or a key fails, listening and translation fall back to local when it is available, so the tool does not stop. Keys for Gemini, OpenAI and Anthropic are checked with the provider before saving.
- 2026-09-27 — Monthly chat subscriptions (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro) cannot be used by other programs; only developer API keys can.
- 2026-09-27 — Measured fully local mode (no key, no internet) on the sample: 45 s for one minute. It works as a fallback but is weaker: formal rather than Saudi Arabic, speakers merged in one line because local listening does not tell voices apart, and slightly looser timing. The OpenAI and Anthropic paths are covered by tests with simulated replies; they have not been run against the real services because no keys were available.
- 2026-09-27 — A welcome page served by tarjim itself (http://127.0.0.1:17653/) is both the first-run setup and the app afterwards. Setup has seven steps: interface language (Arabic and English for now, each written in its own script; the page direction follows it), a real device check (graphics card, memory, free space, ffmpeg), the engine (cloud with a free Gemini key recommended, fully local, or advanced per role), only the keys that choice needs (checked with the provider), the tools with sizes (essentials required, the rest optional), the browser extension, and done. Walked through as a brand-new user against a fresh settings folder; a missing key and missing essential tools stop the step with a plain sentence.
- 2026-09-27 — The page talks to the server with an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict session cookie set when the page loads, accepted only from the same origin; the page sends a strict Content-Security-Policy, cannot be framed, and serves only files inside its own folder.
- 2026-09-27 — The extension pairs itself: it asks the server, a request appears on the tarjim page, the person presses Allow, and the extension receives the token once. Requests are accepted only from extension origins, expire after ten minutes and are limited to five at a time. Pasting the token by hand remains as a fallback.
- 2026-09-27 — The page and the extension share the same visual tokens but keep their own stylesheets, because they are shipped separately.
- 2026-09-27 — The right-click menu keeps the shape people already know from the old tool: "ترجم للعربية" with subtitle file, subtitles in the video, and the four dubbing choices under it. When another target language is picked in the popup, the title follows it ("ترجم إلى English") and the Arabic narrator is hidden. A last item opens the full page for another language or a file on the computer. Menu rebuilds are serialised so a startup and an install event cannot create it twice. Checked by running the menu code under a simulated Chrome for Arabic and English.
- 2026-09-27 — Pairing survives the popup closing. The first version waited for approval inside the popup, which Chrome closes the moment the person switches to the tarjim page to press Allow, so pairing never finished (seen on the owner's machine: two requests left pending). Now the pairing request id is stored, the extension opens the tarjim page itself, and both the popup and the background alarm claim the token once it is allowed, with a notification.
- 2026-09-27 — The extension follows the interface language chosen in tarjim, not the browser's language. The server answers `/ui-language` without a token (it reveals only that choice), the extension loads its own message file for that language and sets the direction, and the background reloads it whenever it wakes up.

The server now keeps the aligner, Qwen3 and XTTS loaded after first use, and saves the converted fast tokenizer next to the model once.

- 2026-09-27 — Downloaded originals go to a hidden `~/.tarjim/sources` folder and only the results land in Downloads/tarjim, so the untranslated original is not mistaken for the result. Progress shows a separate Timing stage after Listening and how long a job has been running.
- 2026-09-27 — Keys and the security token are stored encrypted in the operating system's own vault (Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain, the Secret Service on Linux) through `keyring`; the settings file keeps only ordinary choices. Keys found in an older settings file move into the vault at startup and are removed from the file. When a machine has no vault the owner-only file is the fallback, and the documentation must say so rather than claim encryption. Tests always use an in-memory vault so they can never touch real keys.
- 2026-09-27 — Jobs can be paused, resumed and cancelled. The worker checks between steps, so a pause takes effect when the current step ends; a cancelled job can be retried.
- 2026-09-27 — tarjim is also an MCP server (`tarjim-mcp`, stdio) so it can be added to Claude like any other service and used in chat: translate a link or a local file, follow and control jobs, read and change settings, list languages. Now a pass with a line over 60 s or faster than 8 words/s is left out of the vote (all passes kept if none is sane); the cached passes are re-voted when heard.json is read, so a retry benefits; a final timing with over 30% of words shorter than 20 ms or faster than 8 words/s over the speech is replaced by the local listener's words when those are sane, and such a transcript is never reused from the cache.

- 2026-10-31 — Burns enlarge pictures under 1080p before libass draws (`render/sharp.py`): fit inside 1920×1080 (1080×1920 portrait), lanczos, skipped when the factor is under 1.1. The size comes from the .ass file's own PlayRes, so nothing else changes: libass maps PlayRes coordinates, `\pos`, margins and (with ScaledBorderAndShadow) outline and shadow onto the frame it gets; only tarjim's logo, overlaid in pixels, is scaled by the same factor (`sharp.grown`). The rate ceiling is multiplied by the factor (linear, not by area: the enlarged picture holds no new detail, the extra bits go to the letters' edges). Measured on the 848×464 Tsar Bomba clip (H.264 267 kb/s): output 1920×1050, 674 kb/s, 2.9 → 6.1 MB, letters crisp at native size where the 464p burn was soft. Cost on weak hardware: the encode handles ~5× the pixels (fine on any GPU encoder; OpenH264 on a CPU is still faster than real time at 1080p). The phone copy still scales to ≤1280 wide.
